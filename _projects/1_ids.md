Cyber-physical systems — vehicles, industrial controllers, medical devices —
are built from dozens of embedded computers supplied by different vendors. Very
little in the deployed system verifies that each of those components is the one
that was originally installed.

This project builds intrusion detection that answers that question from the
hardware itself. Manufacturing variation makes every chip physically distinct,
and those differences are measurable. We use them as a fingerprint: a device
that has been swapped, counterfeited, or tampered with does not match the
fingerprint recorded for it.

The detection runs on an FPGA inside the system, in real time, rather than
depending on a network connection to a remote analyser.

**Funding:** National Science Foundation
**Lab:** Information Systems, Security, and Forensics (ISSF), UM-Dearborn
